# Endurance Nutrition — Daily Eating Rules

> Source: IRONMAN — "Eat Like An IRONMAN: 6 Nutrition Rules For Endurance
> Athletes" (ironman.com). 10+ h·wk⁻¹ athletes: nutrition guidance gets
> muddy fast — these are the back-to-basics rules from sports nutritionists.

## Rule 1 — Eat a quality daily diet
- General health is the foundation of endurance fitness; a high-quality diet
  is essential for general health.
- Quality food satisfies a training-inflated appetite in a calorically
  efficient way — most triathletes struggle to get leaner despite heavy
  training.
- Score your diet (e.g. USDA MyPlate, or the Diet Quality Score in Matt
  Fitzgerald's "Racing Weight").
- Nailed it when: plate is full of fruits, vegetables, lean protein and
  complex carbohydrates; junk food is avoided (including large amounts of
  processed energy bars and gels); fatty foods limited; alcohol at one to two
  drinks a day max; caffeine kept to a minimum.

## Rule 2 — Practice meal timing
- Have some nutrition roughly 1–3 hours before a training session.
- Short/high-intensity sessions under 2 hours: fewer carbs can teach the body
  to rely on fat stores; liquid-based nutrition (sports drink) suits these.
- Sessions over 3 hours: 200–300 g of carbohydrate 1–4 hours beforehand.
- Post-workout: the 30–60 minutes right after matters most for workouts longer
  than 2 hours or high-intensity ones. Consume 1–1.2 g carbohydrate per kg
  body weight plus 10–25 g protein. Fat inhibits carbohydrate absorption for a
  few hours after — keep the recovery feed lean.
- Red flags: GI distress in workouts (often too many carbs); low energy,
  heavy or sluggish before/during workouts.
- Nailed it when: satiated, energetic and light in every workout regardless of
  time of day.

## Rule 3 — Monitor macronutrients
- Daily targets for endurance athletes: 45–65% of calories from carbohydrate,
  15–20% from protein, 20–35% from fat.
- Longer-course athletes (IRONMAN) need slightly more carbohydrate than
  short-course triathletes logging fewer hours — carbohydrate is the body's
  primary energy source.
- Red flags: low energy before/during/after workouts; frequent fatigue; slow
  recovery especially after high-intensity sessions; prone to illness and
  injury.

## Rule 4 — Eat enough (daily total)
- Many endurance athletes fuel workouts properly but finish the day in a
  caloric deficit — under-fed triathletes are common in this sport.
- "Performance starts with fuel" — training is not the missing link, nutrition
  is.
- An 800–1000 calorie breakfast is fine — split across pre-workout, during and
  after, it's not hard to do. The first meal should be a third to half of
  daily calories.
- Most people eat in a crescendo (small breakfast, huge dinner) — prefer an
  even pattern of moderately-sized frequent meals. Trust hunger messages: the
  body is the best calorie counter.
- Red flags: workouts feel low quality; thinking about food all the time;
  evening hunger spikes.
- Nailed it when: performance keeps improving, recovery is quick, sweet
  cravings are infrequent (people who feel addicted to sugar are often just
  hungry).
