# Endurance Hydration & Food Mindset

> Source: IRONMAN — "Eat Like An IRONMAN: 6 Nutrition Rules For Endurance
> Athletes" (ironman.com). Rules 5–6 of the endurance nutrition set — see
> endurance_nutrition_daily.md for rules 1–4.

## Rule 5 — Hydrate smart (daily, not just race day)
- Hydration is the balance of fluids AND electrolytes — minerals essential to
  sports performance and overall health (digestion, nutrient absorption,
  skin, brain function all rely on it).
- The "eight glasses a day" idea has no scientific basis. Needs vary by body
  size, sweat rate, activity level, weather and altitude.
- Pay attention to thirst rather than a rigid plan.
- Red flags: fatigue, nausea, dry lips and throat, frequent headaches, reduced
  concentration; OR urinating very frequently with water-coloured urine; OR
  hardly urinating with dark yellow urine.
- Nailed it when: aware of daily needs based on work and climate; hydrating
  with low-sugar electrolyte drinks through the day to top up salts and
  minerals; urine is consistently straw-coloured yellow.

## Rule 6 — Maintain a healthy relationship with food
- Eating should be healthful AND pleasurable. Endurance athletes tend to be
  obsessive — logging hours and monitoring bodies like science experiments can
  spill into denial and guilt around food.
- Self-check questions: Do you eat behind a screen or in the car? Do you use
  silverware? Do you skip meals? Are you afraid to eat around others? Do you
  have an off-limits food list? Does food make you feel guilty?
- If any answer is yes, work on mindful eating: a real-food, balanced diet
  with three meals and purposeful snacking — no dieting, no 'cheating', no
  off-limits lists.
- Nailed it when: eating mindfully, consuming real whole foods, avoiding
  pre-packaged convenience food, and preparing meals at home more often than
  eating out.
