# Triathlon Training by Distance

> Source: T100 Triathlon — "How to Train for Triathlons: Everything You Need
> to Know" (t100triathlon.com/articles/training/how-to-train-for-triathlons/)

## What shapes training time
- **Fitness level** — new to all three sports: build an endurance base first.
  A strong runner with no swim background needs far more pool time than a
  runner with swimming history.
- **Race distance** — sprint = weeks of prep; full IRONMAN = a year-long
  endeavour; T100/100 km sits between: a few months is enough, more makes you
  faster.
- **Goals** — first finish, personal best, or podium all demand different
  volumes.

## Typical timelines
- Sprint triathlon: 6–12 weeks for most beginners.
- Olympic triathlon: 8–16 weeks depending on experience.
- T100 or 70.3: 16–24 weeks to develop endurance and race strategy.
- Full IRONMAN: 6–12 months of disciplined, progressive training.
- Build gradually over weeks to avoid injury so you arrive with energy in the
  tank.

## The 10% rule
- Do not increase distance or duration in any single discipline by more than
  10% per week — gives the body time to adapt.
- Applies discipline-by-discipline (swim, bike, run each counted separately).

## Triathlon 101 facts (for beginners)
- A reliable bike, well-fitting tri-suit, goggles and running shoes are enough
  to start. Advanced gear adds efficiency or free speed — not essential.
- Swim is the biggest blocker for most beginners — fix it with a tri club,
  coached swim group, or technique drills. Non-swimmers can absolutely race.
- Most triathletes train 5–6 days a week with at least one full rest or active
  recovery day.
- Transitions and the bike-to-run shift challenge newcomers — practise them
  (see race_day_prep.md).
- Golden rule: progress, not perfection. Celebrate small wins — a 1 km
  non-stop swim, a first 30 km bike, running without pausing.

## Niggle rule (when to back off)
- If a niggle appears during training and persists without improvement for a
  couple of days, take a few days off from that sport.
- Attuning to your body's signals beats pushing through and losing weeks to
  injury.
