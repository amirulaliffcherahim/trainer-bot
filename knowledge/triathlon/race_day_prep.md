# Triathlon Race-Day Prep: Bricks, Open Water, Taper

> Source: T100 Triathlon — "How to Train for Triathlons: Everything You Need
> to Know" (t100triathlon.com/articles/training/how-to-train-for-triathlons/)

## Brick sessions (bike → run)
- Purpose: get used to the 'jelly-legged' feeling of moving from cycling to
  running, and discipline run pacing — pace is harder to judge right off the
  bike.
- A brick is simply a quick transition from one sport to another, typically
  bike to run.
- Sprint-distance bricks teach the same skill with less time cost.

## Open-water swimming
- Never let race day be your first open-water (or wetsuit) swim of the year —
  a few sessions in open water removes the shock factor.
- Enter the water slowly to avoid a chilly shock.

## Race-week taper
- Last couple of weeks before the event: reduce training volume. You will not
  get fitter at this point — you can only overdo it and arrive fatigued.
- You're better off being 10% underdone than 1% overdone. No last-minute
  'panic training'.

## Race strategy
- Practise pacing in training so you don't burn all your energy on the bike
  and suffer on the run.
- Fuel during training — it teaches your gut what it can handle on race day.
- Time budget by discipline (approx, T100/100 km): swim ~15%, bike ~45%, run
  ~36%, remainder transitions. Use it to balance what you enjoy vs what you
  need.

## Session hygiene
- Warm up every session — eases heart and muscles in, cuts injury risk.
- Endurance workouts that mimic race conditions are the essential long work.
- See training_by_distance.md for the niggle rule and 10% progression cap.
