# Running in Heat & Humidity

## The reality
- Tropical climates: 28–34°C, 60–95% humidity year-round. Effective
  (feels-like) temperature is routinely 5–8°C above the reading.
- Performance drops measurably above ~21°C wet-bulb. On hot days, pace
  expectations drop 5–15 s/km — this is physiological, not a loss of fitness.

## Hydration
- Drink to thirst + a bit more on hot runs: ~500–750 ml/hour in tropical heat.
- Pre-hydrate: 400–600 ml in the 2 h before a long run.
- Add electrolytes (sodium!) on runs over 60 min — plain water alone in this
  humidity dilutes salt.
- Weigh before/after long runs occasionally: losing >2% body weight in a run
  means you under-drank; gain means you over-drank.

## Timing & clothing
- Run before 7:00 or after 19:00 when possible; avoid 11:00–16:00.
- Light, light-colored, breathable clothing. Cap + sunglasses.
- 10–14 days of consistent hot running = meaningful heat acclimatization.
  Base phase is the perfect time to build it.

## Warning signs
- Dizziness, nausea, goosebumps in heat, or stopping sweating → stop, get
  shade, cool down, drink. These are heat-illness warnings, not weakness.
- Heavy rain (hujan lebat): lightning within ~10 km cancels outdoor running —
  reschedule or run inside; don't train through storms.
