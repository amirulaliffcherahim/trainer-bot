# Productive Weekly Training Program (Runner)

> Source: Strava Stories, Coach Nick Bester — "A Productive Weekly Training
> Program for Runners" (stories.strava.com)

## The 8 training tips
1. **Keep hard days hard and easy days easy.** Snap out of the comfort zone
   on hard days; let the body absorb the work by keeping easy days truly easy.
2. **Maximum 2.5 harder days per week.** More than that and training stops
   being productive — you cannot push hard every day.
3. **Improvement requires discomfort.** The more you leave your comfort zone,
   the more used to the feeling you get.
4. **Fitness compounds slowly.** Consistency over time; don't get disheartened
   when results don't come immediately.
5. **Fuel hard to train hard.** Good carbs (wholewheat pasta, brown rice,
   sweet potato) the night before hard sessions; ≥20–24 g protein within 30
   minutes after a session, then a bigger meal about an hour later.
6. **More speed → more minor injuries — train smart.** If a niggle is worse
   than 6–7/10, back off sooner rather than later and cross-train (spin bike,
   swimming, elliptical, rowing). See a physio — professionals diagnose
   injuries.
7. **Strength & conditioning keeps you injury-free.** Muscles must develop to
   sustain training demands; don't neglect S&C for extra running.
8. **Get a coach or follow structure.** Structure pays; low-cost alternatives
   exist when coaching isn't affordable.

## The productive week
Pattern: hard days are really hard (max 2.5/week), easy days are really easy,
and every week carries one full rest day. Effort ratings are 1–10.

### Monday — easy (3/10)
Bulk of training lives here: ~75–80% of weekly volume. Heart rate: stay in
zone 2, average HR ≤ 72% of max. Easy runs have no pace — conversational the
whole way.

### Tuesday — hard (9/10)
Track/interval session at race pace and faster. Example: 10 × 400 m (75 s
recovery) @ target 5k pace, starting at 85% effort and finishing at 95%.
Recovery: static, walk or easy jog. Training with a group makes hard sessions
easier.

### Wednesday — easy (3/10)
Recovery from Tuesday. Good day for cross-training (spin bike, swimming,
elliptical, rowing) if the body needs a break from running impact.

### Thursday — intermediate or easy
Beginners: another easy day. Experienced and feeling good: a 'half' hard day
(6–7/10) — hill work or tempo work.
- Hill work builds strength, form and power — "hills pay the bills" even for
  flat races. Get elevation into the week.
- Tempo: harder than normal but not racing — a few words but not a full
  sentence. Stay controlled.

### Friday — rest day from cardio (1/10)
Full rest lets the body absorb training. Yoga, or a stretching sequence
focused on where you're tightest — for runners often hips and hamstrings.

### Saturday — quality or easy (8/10 or 4/10)
Option A: quality session — then Sunday's long run stays easy. Option B: easy
run — then Sunday carries a harder structured long run. Quality work here is
very hard but not as hard as Tuesday's interval session.

### Sunday — long run (opposite of Saturday)
If Saturday was hard, keep the long run easy — mind drifts, plod away. If
Saturday was easy, make the long run harder with structure — e.g. 3 × 10 min
at effort inside the run, or 3–4 × 5k efforts when marathon-building.

## Core message
- Stay consistent — the hardest part is the beginning; keep showing up and the
  body adapts. Train smart, be productive.
