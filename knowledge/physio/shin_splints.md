# Shin Splints (MTSS) — Load Management & Rehab

## What it is (plainly)
- Medial tibial stress syndrome: overload of the shin bone and its muscle
  attachments, typically from a rapid volume increase, hard surfaces, or
  overstriding. Diffuse ache along the inner shin.

## Distinguish from a stress fracture
- MTSS: diffuse ache along the inner third of the shin, eases as the run
  progresses, both legs often.
- Stress fracture: pinpoint pain on the bone, worsens as the run progresses,
  painful to hop on that leg. Pinpoint + hopping pain = stop and get an X-ray.

## What to do
- Immediate: cut volume 30–50% and eliminate the aggravating surface (no
  concrete, no track). Substitute cycling or easy cross-training to hold
  fitness.
- **Calf raises** (straight + bent knee) 3×15 daily — the primary rehab load.
- Strengthen the foot: towel scrunches / short-foot holds daily.
- Walk and run on softer surfaces; shorten stride slightly (quicker cadence,
  ~175+ steps/min) to reduce shin impact.
- Ice the inner shin 10 min after runs if swollen or tender (not mandatory).

## Return-to-run rules
- Pain-free walking for 3 days → add easy running at 50% volume, 1 min run /
  1 min walk intervals if needed.
- Progress volume by ≤10%/week ONLY while the shin stays pain-free during AND
  after the run (24 h check).
- Recurrence means the base volume is too high: drop 20% and rebuild.

## When to see a professional
- Pinpoint bone pain, night pain, or pain on hopping → stress fracture
  suspicion, stop running entirely.
- No improvement after 3 weeks of consistent load management.
