# Quadriceps Tendonitis — Load Management & Rehab

## What it is (plainly)
- Overload of the quad tendon where it meets the kneecap. Common in runners
  with a big weekly step-up in volume or hill/speed work. Prevention beats
  rehab: consistent load progression plus the quad stretch habit.

## Warning signs
- Pain at the bottom of the kneecap, worse with stairs, squats, or after
  sitting (startup pain).
- Morning stiffness in the knee that loosens with movement.
- Tenderness on the tendon itself when pressing.

## Immediate protocol (flare / strain)
- **Ice 15 min, 2–3×/day** over the painful area.
- **Foam roll the upper and middle quads ONLY** — never directly on the
  painful tendon spot above the kneecap.
- **Skip knee-loading exercises** (squats, lunges, Bulgarian split squats —
  the high eccentric load is a known trigger for this athlete).
- **Extra rest if stairs provoke pain** — stairs are the honest test; if
  stairs hurt, running is off the table.

## What to do
- Stop the aggravating loading: no squats to depth, no steep hills, no
  sprinting. Running on the flat at easy pace can continue at 0–2/10 pain.
- **Isometric phase (first):** wall-sit or quarter-squat holds — 5×45 s,
  twice daily, at pain ≤3/10. Isometrics are the fastest pain relief.
- **Eccentric phase (after 1–2 weeks pain-free):** slow decline squats on
  both legs (3×10, 3 s lowering), progressing to single-leg as tolerated.
- Keep the quad stretch 2×45 s/side but never stretch into pain.

## Load rules
- Resume hills/speed only after: pain-free isometrics AND pain-free stairs.
- Then re-introduce ONE quality session per week and watch the 24 h response.
- Volume cap still applies (≤10%/week) — tendon loading counts double:
  a hill session counts like two flat sessions.

## When to see a professional
- No improvement after 2 weeks of consistent isometrics.
- Night pain, swelling, or pain at rest.
- Any sharp catching sensation in the knee.
