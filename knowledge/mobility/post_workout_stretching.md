# Post-Workout Stretching — Safe & Effective

> Source: Mayo Clinic — "Stretching: Focus on flexibility" (mayoclinic.org,
> Nov 2023). Substitutes the Technogym post-workout stretching article, which
> blocks external fetches (HTTP 403).

## What stretching does (and doesn't) do
- Research is mixed: stretching does not reliably reduce muscle soreness after
  exercise, and static stretching right before sprinting may slightly worsen
  performance.
- What it does: improves flexibility and joint range of motion. Better
  flexibility may improve physical performance, decrease injury risk, let
  joints move through full range, increase muscle blood flow, and help muscles
  work effectively.
- Stretching will NOT prevent overuse injuries.

## Safe stretching essentials
- **Don't stretch cold.** Stretch after the workout when muscles are warm, or
  warm up 5–10 minutes first with light walking, jogging or biking.
- **Skip static stretching before intense/sprint activity** — pre-event
  stretching may decrease performance and weaken hamstring strength.
- **Aim for symmetry, not depth.** Genetics set your flexibility; equal
  flexibility side-to-side matters most, especially with an injury history.
- **Focus on major muscle groups** used in your sport (runners: calves,
  thighs, hips, lower back, hamstrings) — stretch both sides.
- **Don't bounce** — bounce-stretching can injure muscle and add tightness.
  Stretch in smooth movements.
- **Hold it.** Breathe normally; hold ~30 seconds per stretch; problem areas
  up to ~60 seconds.
- **Tension, not pain.** If it hurts, you've gone too far — back off to
  pain-free and hold there.
- **Make stretches sport-specific** — stretch the muscles your sport stresses
  most (e.g. hamstrings for anyone sprinting, given strain risk).
- **Keep it regular** — 2–3 times a week minimum to keep benefits; even 5–10
  minutes a session helps. Stop stretching and range of motion drifts back.

## Movement-based alternatives
- Tai chi, Pilates and yoga improve flexibility through movement.
- A dynamic warmup (sport-specific movements at low level, building speed and
  intensity) suits pre-workout.
- Foam rolling before a workout alongside a dynamic warmup is an option some
  athletes add.

## When to be cautious
- Chronic conditions or injury: adjust technique — a strained muscle can be
  further harmed by stretching it. Ask a doctor or physical therapist what is
  appropriate.
